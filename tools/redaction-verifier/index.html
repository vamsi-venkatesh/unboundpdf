Redaction Verifier

Check any redacted PDF for text still hiding under the boxes.

Redaction Verifier checks a redacted PDF from any tool for text that is still present underneath the black boxes — text operators under a filled cover, invisible text, hidden layers, and annotation-only covers that a reader can delete or hide — and reports page by page with the positions of what it found (the text itself is hidden until you choose to reveal it). It detects text that is still present; it cannot see what was removed, and a clean result is not evidence that a redaction was performed. Pages drawn through a form XObject are reported as could not check, never as clear.

Redaction Verifier answers one question about a PDF: is the text really gone? It reads the file's own content and reports text that is still there — sitting under a filled box, drawn invisibly, held in a layer the document hides, or covered only by an annotation a reader can delete. It detects text that is still present; it cannot see what was removed.

How to use Redaction Verifier — steps & common uses

What people use Redaction Verifier for

Anyone about to send out a redacted document, and anyone who has received one and wants to know what it still contains.

  • Check a redacted file before sending it out
  • Test whether another tool's redaction actually removed the text
  • Find invisible text left in a document
  • Spot an annotation used as a redaction
  • Produce a per-page record of what was found

How to use Redaction Verifier

  1. Add the PDF. It can come from any tool, ours or anyone else's, and it is read on your device.
  2. Choose which pages to check, or leave the whole document selected.
  3. Click Check this document. Each page gets its own verdict, with the position of anything found.
  4. Reveal the text only if you need to see it, and download the report — masked by default.

Good to know: Detects text that is still present; a result with no findings is not evidence that a redaction was performed.

Questions about Redaction Verifier 7
Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. UnboundPDF processes PDFs locally in the browser — documents are not uploaded to a server. We have no backend that receives, stores, or scans your files.

What does a result with no findings mean?

It means no extractable text was found under a mark on the pages that could be read. It is not evidence that a redaction was performed, and it is not a statement about anything this check cannot see.

What exactly does it look for?

Text under a filled rectangle drawn after it, text drawn in rendering mode 3 so it paints nothing, text in an optional-content layer the document asks readers to hide, and text under a Square, Redact, Highlight, FreeText, Ink, Stamp or Polygon annotation.

Why is the text hidden until I click?

Because the findings are the very strings someone tried to remove. They stay masked on screen and in the downloaded report until you ask for them.

Is invisible text always a failed redaction?

No, and the tool says so. A searchable scan carries an invisible OCR layer by design, which looks the same. It is reported as its own kind of finding rather than counted as a cover.

Are there pages it cannot check?

Yes. Text drawn inside a Form XObject, as vector outlines, or with a Type 3 font is not readable as text. Those pages are reported as "could not check" and never as clear.

Will it flag an ordinary black table cell?

It can. A filled rectangle over text has the same shape whatever it was drawn for, so the finding names the position and lets you judge it.

How your files are handled

The work runs on your own device, inside the browser tab — so there is no server to send your document to.

  • Nothing is uploaded. We have no backend that receives or stores documents, so we cannot see your files during processing or after.
  • Your device does the work. Large documents are handled in stages, pages are rendered as you reach them, and each job is checked against your device before it starts — so a long run takes longer on a phone than on a laptop, nothing is queued behind other people's jobs, and you can stop a run at any point without touching your original file.
